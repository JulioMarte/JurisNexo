# Adversarial legal scenarios

These scenarios are the semantic fixtures the V4 schema must continue to represent. Tests may encode them across multiple files as long as the guarantees remain executable.

## Dominican multi-stage litigation

- first-instance proceeding with multiple claims and a counterclaim;
- appeal in which one claim is abandoned and another narrowed;
- partial cassation;
- remand to a different court organ;
- subsequent decision and new review proceeding;
- cross-instance claim lineage remains explicit.

## Consolidated decision

Three proceedings are connected to one judicial decision without choosing a fictitious single legal proceeding as the decision's identity.

## Mixed collegiate position

A panel contains judges who join the result, dissent on a proposition, and save a vote on part of the disposition. Panel membership, authored opinion, vote and scoped stance remain distinct.

## Complex dispositive grammar

One disposition may:

- reject one claim;
- grant another;
- cassate only part of a prior decision;
- remand the affected matter;
- name a destination court/organ;
- impose or compensate costs;
- express an obligor, beneficiary, amount/currency, object and scope without flattening those roles into one target.

## Allegation versus finding

A party alleges that notice never occurred. The court may later find the same fact proved, reject it, or make no finding. The allegation and finding remain separate identities even when wording is identical.

## Contradictory historical sources

An index, bulletin header, OCR extraction and later official registry disagree on a decision date. Every source observation remains preserved; the current canonical resolution records its basis without deleting conflicting evidence.

## Entity ambiguity

`J. Pérez` may refer to more than one candidate person. Name similarity creates candidates, not a canonical merge. A later evidenced resolution preserves the original observed identity and knowledge history.
